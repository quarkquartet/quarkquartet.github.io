The Universe provides a way to study new particles through their effects on cosmic evolution and the signals that reach us from astrophysical sources.
The abundance of dark matter, the matter–antimatter asymmetry, and the properties of cosmic messengers can all carry information about physics beyond the Standard Model.

My research follows three directions: axion cosmology, dark matter and the cosmic thermal history, and astrophysical probes of new physics.

## Axion cosmology

The axion was proposed to explain why strong interactions preserve charge–parity (CP) symmetry to such high precision, a puzzle known as the strong CP problem.
The attracting part is that axion can also be a well-motivated dark matter candidate.

Traditionally, people think axion dark matter is produced via misalignment mechanism, where the axion field was at a static value away from the potential mininum at the beginning of the universe, and then starts oscillating around the minimum.
Recently, it's been proposed that axion field may have a large initial rotating kinetic energy, known as [kinetic misalignment mechnism](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.14152).
This initial rotation might also help achieving baryogenesis, known as [axiogenesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02080).

These possibilities motivate a broader study of axion dynamics and their connections to dark matter and baryogenesis.
On the one hand, we need a good particle physics model to achieve the right cosmology of rotating axion and axiogenesis. On the other hand, we would like to know how to distinguish whether the axion is initially rotating or at rest.

My work explores along these directions.
For example, axiogenesis and dark matter could be co-explained by utilizing a [high-scale $SU(2)_R$ phase transition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09679). This serves as one of the earliest work connecting axiogenesis and high-scale phase transition.
We have shown that [interactions with dark magnetic monopoles can diminish over-produced axion dark matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10603).

## Dark matter and the cosmic thermal history

Besides axion, there are a few other possibilities of dark matter production in the early universe, including freeze-out mechanism, freeze-in mechanism, or something else.

With the dark matter production, the thermal history of our universe could be modified.
Their production, annihilation, and decay can redistribute energy among the particles in the cosmic plasma, while phase transitions can change the state of matter and the interactions that govern its evolution.
These changes can leave signatures in the primordial abundances of light elements, the cosmic microwave background, and the relic neutrino population.
Cosmological observations thus allow us to test new particles through their effects on the early Universe.

My work includes [thermal dark matter coupled to quarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03133) and [light dark matter interacting with cosmic neutrinos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17151).

## Astrophysical probes of new physics

Astrophysical sources produce particles over a wide range of energies and send them across enormous distances.
Neutrinos and cosmic rays provide information about these sources and the interactions encountered along their journey.
New particles or forces can change the signals that reach Earth, making astrophysical observations a way to probe fundamental physics.

Neutrinos are particularly useful because their weak interactions allow them to travel through dense environments and over cosmic distances.
Even small additional interactions can become relevant over such long journeys and modify the observed energy spectrum.
Cosmic rays provide another opportunity: some reach energies far beyond those available in laboratory particle beams.
Their collisions with matter can produce new particles, allowing the Universe to act as a natural accelerator.

My work uses [supernova neutrinos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07624) and [ultra-high-energy neutrinos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00165) to probe new neutrino interactions.
